Overview

The contexts of use of the model, their model risk from the influence of the model on the decision and the consequence of a wrong decision, and their verdict on the decisive evidence (decided on): the calibration data, the data the model was fitted to, for a description (C1), else the validation data. The evaluable points are the decisive points which the fold criteria can evaluate; a verdict needs at least the minimum of them, by default 5 for a low risk, 10 for a medium risk and 20 for a high risk.

Limitations of the model

  • limitations: HCTZ data of processes which the model lacks: the patients with an impairment of Anderson1961 (no measured cardiac output or creatinine clearance, cirrhosis has no effect in the model, case 13 has lupus), the feces after intravenous doses of Beermann1976 (no non-renal elimination), the urine volumes of the water load of Beermann1977a (no water diuresis) and the single subject of Knauf1995 Fig1 (no variability between subjects) (77 points of 4 studies)

Notes

Renal impairment is claimed (`renal`) and calibrated: the impaired arms are training data by the decision of the maintainer, so the COU has one independent datum and reaches no verdict. Not claimed: hepatic and cardiac impairment (the impaired patients of Anderson1961 are a limitation, the single subjects of Beermann1979 are not described), the held-out hypertensive patients of Beermann1976 (50 mg, multiple dosing), multiple dosing without a coadministration, the terminal phase of the plasma beyond 24 hr, the urine volume and the blood pressure. The open points of HCTZ are in its TODO.md.

renal Impaired renal function insufficient evidence

Which plasma concentration and urinary excretion of HCTZ, and which urinary excretion of sodium, follow from an oral dose in patients with an impaired renal function?

level
C3 extrapolate with data
influence
medium
consequence
medium

medium

validation evidence
decisive points
10
evaluable points
10 (minimum 10)
rationale
C3: the renal function of a patient scales the GFR and the activity of SLC22A6, so the model extrapolates the exposure of an impaired kidney from the healthy one; a creatinine clearance which no study gave rests on that extrapolation (influence medium), and HCTZ loses its diuretic effect below a creatinine clearance of about 30 ml/min, which the natriuresis has to reproduce for the statement to carry (consequence medium). The impaired arms are training data by decision: the renal patients of Niemeyer1983 in the PK fit and the patients of Knauf1995 in the PD fit are calibration, not validation, so this COU rests on the fits it is assessed against. Its only independent datum is the held-out patient of Niemeyer1983, far below the minimum of a medium risk, so the verdict is `insufficient_evidence` by construction and is kept: the minimum is not lowered and the claim is not narrowed. The COU needs data of an impaired kidney which the fits do not use; the impaired patients of Anderson1961 cannot serve, they are a limitation (no measured creatinine clearance or cardiac output).

rationale
C5: the PD of the fluid and electrolyte model, driven by the simulated PK. The sodium and chloride of a dose without PD data come from the model, the dose-response of the fitted studies is the check (influence medium); the natriuresis explains why HCTZ is a diuretic, its dose is titrated on the blood pressure (consequence low). Only sodium and chloride are claimed: the validation data of the urine volume (Beermann1977a, a water load) is a limitation and the blood pressure has no validation data (Jeon2012 is training data). The evidence is too thin for a verdict: two evaluable points, the sodium and the chloride of one dose of one study, below the minimum of 5 points of a low risk, so the COU is `insufficient_evidence` although both points are within 2-fold; the calibration has a bias besides, an AFE of 0.68 (0.72 in healthy adults), i.e. the model predicts about 30 % less electrolyte excretion than the data it was fitted to. The risk stays low, since it follows from the decision the answer informs and not from the evidence, and the minimum is not lowered: the COU needs PD validation data of further doses or studies.
