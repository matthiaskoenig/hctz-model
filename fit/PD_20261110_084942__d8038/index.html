Objective function

The parameters were estimated by minimizing the objective function

where k runs over the fit mappings of the training data and i over their data points, ri,k is the residual, wi,k=wkwi,kp the weight of the data point and ρ the loss function, applied to the squared weighted residual z=wi,kri,k2. The residuals are normalized by the mean y―k of the data of their curve, which makes curves of different quantities and magnitudes comparable:

ri,k=yi,k−fk(xi,k,p→)y―k(2)

The weight of curve k is wk=nk/Nk, the weight nk of its fit mapping (the weight its fit mapping collection assigns, 1 by default, or, for a collection which uses the weights of its mappings, the weight of the mapping, by default the number of subjects of its data) divided by the number Nk of its data points.

A data point is weighted with the inverse of its coefficient of variation, wi,kp=|yi,k|/σi,k, with σi,k the standard deviation of the data point, or its standard error for data without a standard deviation. An error of zero is missing, a missing error of a curve with errors is replaced by the largest error of the curve, and the data points of a curve without errors have the weight wi,kp=2, the weight of a coefficient of variation of 0.5.

The objective was minimized in 48 runs with the trust region reflective method [2] of the least squares optimizer of SciPy [3], with a Jacobian of finite forward differences with the relative step 0.001. A run stopped at the tolerances of the change of the cost, of the parameters and of the gradient, ftol = 10−8, xtol = 10−8 and gtol = 10−8.

The start values of the runs were drawn by latin hypercube sampling [4] log-uniformly, i.e. uniformly in the logarithm of the parameters, within their bounds, with the seed 1234.

The convergence of the runs was assessed from the traces of their cost and the waterfall plot of their final costs, and the parameter set with the lowest cost was used.

The models were simulated with libRoadRunner [5] and its integrator CVODE [6] with the relative tolerance 10−6 and the absolute tolerance 10−6, which for a species in a concentration is multiplied by the initial volume of its compartment. It ran with the variable step size of libRoadRunner, i.e. with an output at every step of the integrator. Identifiability

The identifiability of the parameters was analysed with the profile likelihood [10, 11]. The profile of a parameter is the lowest objective with the parameter fixed:

PL(pj)=minpl≠jF(p→)(7)

At every point of a scan the other parameters were re-optimized from the previous point with the least squares optimizer of SciPy with the relative step 0.001. A scan ran from the optimum in both directions in the space of the optimizer, with steps in decades of the parameter, starting at 0.1. A step which raised the objective by more than 0.2 of the distance between the objective at the optimum and the threshold was reduced by the factor 2 and repeated, down to 0.01, and the step after one which raised it by less than that divided by the factor was enlarged by the factor, up to 1. A scan stopped at the threshold, at the bound of the parameter, at a point which failed or after 30 points.

The confidence intervals are the values at which the profile crosses the threshold of the likelihood ratio test

Fthr=Fmin+χα2(df)2(8)

with Fmin the lowest objective of the fit and of all profiles and χα2(df) the quantile of the χ2 distribution at the confidence level α=0.95 with df = 1 degrees of freedom, i.e. 1.92 above the lowest objective. The threshold has this confidence level if F is the negative log-likelihood of the data up to a constant, i.e. if the weighted residuals are independent and normally distributed with unit variance; otherwise the level is approximate. A crossing is interpolated between two points of the profile, on the logarithm of a parameter on a logarithmic scale. A parameter is identifiable if its profile reaches the threshold within the scan on both sides of the optimum, i.e. its confidence interval is finite; practically non-identifiable towards the lower bound if its profile reaches the threshold above the optimum but not below it within the scan, which ends at the lower bound or after 30 points; practically non-identifiable towards the upper bound if its profile reaches the threshold below the optimum but not above it within the scan, which ends at the upper bound or after 30 points; practically non-identifiable if its profile reaches the threshold on neither side within the scans, which end at the bounds or after 30 points, and is not flat; and structurally non-identifiable if its profile is flat on both sides. A side of a profile is flat if it rises by less than 0.05 of the distance between the lowest objective and the threshold, and a profile with a point which failed or did not converge is not classified. The profile likelihood is preferred over the intervals of the Fisher information, its intervals are invariant under a transformation of the parameters and can be asymmetric [12].

The Fisher information is the local alternative to the profiles: it is the Gauss-Newton approximation of the curvature of the objective at the optimum, from the Jacobian J of the weighted residuals of the training data with respect to the parameters p→ in the space of the optimizer, computed by central finite differences with the step hj=ϵmax(|pj|,1), ϵ=0.01 of the parameter pj:

I=J⊤J,cov=σ2I−1,σ2=2Fn−np(9)

with the number n of data points of the training data and the number np of parameters. The information has rank 5 of 5, the number of its eigenvalues above 10−8 of the largest one, and the condition number 184; the covariance of an information of a lower rank is computed with the pseudo-inverse. The Wald intervals are p^j±t(1+α)/2,n−npSEj with the standard errors SEj=covjj at the confidence level α=0.95, transformed back into the units of the fit parameters.

Identifiability

Fisher information

The curvature of the cost at the parameters, FIM = J'J with the jacobian of the weighted residuals, from which the standard errors and the correlations of the parameters follow. It is a local statement: the intervals are exact for a cost which is a quadratic around the optimum and approximate for a non-linear model, where the profile likelihood is the one to trust. The errors and the intervals are computed in the space the optimizer searches, so an interval on a logarithmic scale is not symmetric around the value.
